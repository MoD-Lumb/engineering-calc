# 8UN thread fit — class 2A bolt / class 2B nut (ASME B1.1)

The 8-thread series (8UN) is the constant-pitch series of ASME B1.1 with
**8 threads per inch** for nominal sizes from 1 in to 6 in (1-8 is also the
UNC size). It is the standard thread for large bolts and studs, e.g. flange
bolting to ASME B16.5 / B18.2.1 at 1-1/8 in and larger. Class **2A** (external)
and **2B** (internal) are the general-purpose classes.

## Basic profile

<Formula tex="P = \dfrac{1}{n} = 0.125\ \text{in}, \qquad H = \dfrac{\sqrt{3}}{2}P = 0.866025\,P" />

<Formula tex="E = D - 0.649519\,P \qquad D_1 = D - 1.082532\,P" />

The basic profile has a flat of P/8 at the major diameter D and a flat of P/4
at the minor diameter D₁. The basic thread engagement height is 5H/8.

## Tolerances (classes 2A and 2B)

Pitch-diameter tolerance of class 2A, with the length of engagement LE taken
equal to the basic major diameter (basis of the B1.1 tables):

<Formula tex="T_{d2} = 0.0015\sqrt[3]{D} + 0.0015\sqrt{L_E} + 0.015\sqrt[3]{P^2}, \qquad L_E = D" />

| Quantity | Class | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Allowance es | 2A | 0.300 · T<sub>d2</sub> |
| Pitch-diameter tolerance T<sub>d2</sub> | 2A | formula above |
| Pitch-diameter tolerance T<sub>D2</sub> | 2B | 1.300 · T<sub>d2</sub> |
| Major-diameter tolerance T<sub>d</sub> | 2A | 0.060 · ∛P² |
| Minor-diameter tolerance T<sub>D1</sub> | 2B | 0.25 P − 0.40 P² (finer than 4 tpi) |

Tolerances and the allowance are rounded to 4 decimal places before they are
applied to the basic sizes, which is how the B1.1 tables are built. Class 2B
minor diameters are published to 3 decimals.

## Limits of size

External thread, class 2A (bolt). Maximum material = maximum size:

<Formula tex="d_{max} = D - es \qquad d_{min} = d_{max} - T_d" />
<Formula tex="d_{2,max} = E - es \qquad d_{2,min} = d_{2,max} - T_{d2}" />
<Formula tex="d_{3,max}\ (\text{UNR, ref.}) = d_{2,max} - 0.577350\,P" />

Internal thread, class 2B (nut). Maximum material = minimum size; there is no allowance:

<Formula tex="D_{1,min} = D_1 \qquad D_{1,max} = D_{1,min} + T_{D1}" />
<Formula tex="D_{2,min} = E \qquad D_{2,max} = E + T_{D2} \qquad D_{min} = D" />

## Material conditions and clearance

| Condition | Bolt (2A) | Nut (2B) |
|---|---|---|
| MMC — maximum material | largest bolt: d max, d₂ max | smallest hole: D₁ min, D₂ min |
| LMC — least material | smallest bolt: d min, d₂ min | largest hole: D₁ max, D₂ max |

The tightest fit is an MMC bolt in an MMC nut. Its pitch-diameter clearance is
exactly the allowance es. The loosest fit is an LMC bolt in an LMC nut:

<Formula tex="c_{min} = D_{2,min} - d_{2,max} = es \qquad c_{max} = D_{2,max} - d_{2,min} = es + T_{d2} + T_{D2}" />

With 60° flanks and the bolt centred, a diametral pitch clearance c gives a total axial play of

<Formula tex="a = c \cdot \tan 30^\circ = 0.57735\, c" />

The radial depth of thread engagement is (d − D₁)/2. It is largest at MMC/MMC
and smallest at LMC/LMC.

## Working load: bolt tension and yield

The yield strength S<sub>y</sub> is a **material property**. It comes from the
bolt material specification as a minimum value that depends on the diameter.
For example, ASTM A193 B7 is 105 ksi up to 2-1/2 in, 95 ksi up to 4 in and
75 ksi up to 7 in.

A threaded rod carries tension on the B1.1 **tensile stress area**. Tensile
tests show a threaded rod breaks at about the same stress as a plain rod whose
diameter is the mean of the pitch diameter and the minor diameter, D − 0.9743/n.
B1.1 gives two forms, one for materials up to 100 ksi tensile strength and one
for materials above it:

<Formula tex="A_s = 0.7854\left(D - \dfrac{0.9743}{n}\right)^2 \quad (S_u \le 100\ \text{ksi})" />

<Formula tex="A_s = \pi\left(\dfrac{E_{s,min}}{2} - \dfrac{0.16238}{n}\right)^2 \quad (S_u > 100\ \text{ksi},\ E_{s,min} = d_{2,min}\ \text{of class 2A})" />

<Formula tex="\sigma = \dfrac{F}{A_s} \qquad F_y = S_y A_s \qquad F_u = S_u A_s \qquad \text{utilisation} = \dfrac{\sigma}{S_y}" />

## Thread stripping

A thread strips when its teeth shear off along a cylinder:

- **Bolt threads** shear at the nut minor diameter D₁.
- **Nut threads** shear at the bolt major diameter d.

The shear area is the number of engaged threads (n·L<sub>E</sub>) times the
circumference of that cylinder, times the tooth width on it. A tooth is P/2
wide at the pitch line, and its width changes by 2·tan 30° per unit of
diameter. That gives the B1.1 / FED-STD-H28 shear areas:

<Formula tex="A_{sb} = \pi n L_E D_1\left[\dfrac{1}{2n} + 0.57735\,(d_2 - D_1)\right]" />

<Formula tex="A_{sn} = \pi n L_E\, d\left[\dfrac{1}{2n} + 0.57735\,(d - D_2)\right]" />

B1.1 evaluates these with least-material limits (D₁ max, d₂ min, d min, D₂ max)
as the worst case. The calculator shows MMC/MMC and LMC/LMC. L<sub>E</sub> is
the nut thickness minus its two 120° countersinks (≈ H<sub>nut</sub> − 0.69 P).
The shear stress is compared with the von Mises shear yield, and the ultimate
stripping load uses τ<sub>u</sub> ≈ 0.6 S<sub>u</sub>:

<Formula tex="\tau = \dfrac{F}{A_{s}} \le 0.577\, S_y \qquad F_{strip,u} = 0.6\, S_u A_s" />

For the bolt to break before the threads strip, B1.1 Appendix B requires at
least this engagement. The nut-thread check uses the same formula, scaled by
the bolt/nut strength ratio.

<Formula tex="L_{E,req} = \dfrac{2 A_s}{\pi n K_{n,max}\left[\dfrac{1}{2n} + 0.57735\,(E_{s,min} - K_{n,max})\right]}" />

Nut specifications such as ASTM A194 give hardness and proof stress, not
yield or tensile strength. The nut values in the presets are therefore
estimates; replace them with certified values.

## Elongation with the nut fixed

The nut is held, and the load pulls the bolt head away from it:

- the plain shank (area A<sub>b</sub> = πD²/4, length L<sub>s</sub>) and the free
  thread (A<sub>s</sub>, length L<sub>t</sub>) carry the full load;
- under the **full-load stretch** assumption the engaged thread (length L<sub>E</sub>)
  is also strained by the full load.

<Formula tex="\delta = \dfrac{F}{E}\left(\dfrac{L_s}{A_b} + \dfrac{L_t}{A_s} + \dfrac{L_E}{A_s}\right)" />

Bolt length and thread length follow ASME B18.2.1. The thread length is
2D + 1/4 in for bolts up to 6 in long and 2D + 1/2 in above that.

## Flank interference under load

Under load the engaged part of the bolt stretches, so its pitch grows by the
strain ε = F/(E·A<sub>s</sub>), while the fixed nut keeps its pitch.

1. The bolt is pulled until the first engaged thread, at the nut bearing face,
   bears on its loaded flank.
2. Further along the nut, each bolt thread lags behind by ε·x. That closes the
   back-flank gap, which starts out as the full axial play a.
3. Poisson contraction makes the bolt thread thinner, which adds play p.

<Formula tex="a = (D_2 - d_2)\tan 30^\circ \qquad p = \nu\,\varepsilon\, d_2 \tan 30^\circ" />

<Formula tex="g_{back}(x) = a + p - \varepsilon x \qquad g_{loaded}(x) = \varepsilon x" />

Interference exists when the back-flank gap at the last engaged thread is
negative. It starts at the load:

<Formula tex="F_{int} = \dfrac{a\, E\, A_s}{L_E - \nu\, d_2 \tan 30^\circ}" />

This is the B1.1 idea that a lead deviation uses up pitch-diameter clearance:
δ<sub>lead</sub>·cot 30° on diameter. It is a **kinematic check**. Real
threads bend and the nut dilates, so the load concentrates on the first few
threads instead of jamming. Interference here marks where the threads must
deform to stay in contact; it does not mean the assembly binds.

Assumptions: static load, no preload, rigid nut, uniform shear over the engaged
threads, and steel E = 29 000 ksi (B8: 28 000 ksi) with ν = 0.3.
VDI 2230 models the head and nut regions more realistically, with substitute
lengths of about 0.4 d to 0.5 d.

## What the model shows

- Threads are true helical 60° UN forms built from the limits above: a flat
  crest and a UNR rounded root on the bolt, a flat root and crest on the nut,
  right-hand, 8 tpi.
- **Tolerance magnification ×k** draws every diameter as
  basic + k·(limit − basic). ×1 is true scale. Clearances of a few
  thousandths of an inch only become visible when they are magnified.
- The section caps are exact axial sections of the helical solids. The MMC
  colour remains visible only where MMC material exceeds LMC material — the
  tolerance zone. The green fill is the free space between the visible bolt and
  nut.
- Values marked † are not toleranced by B1.1: the bolt minor diameter at LMC
  and the nut major diameter at LMC. They are derived from the thread form so
  that the least-material solids are fully defined.
- **Apply load in 3D** stretches the bolt in the vertex shader with the
  displacement field above. The free length is magnified ×M so the elongation
  is visible. The engaged thread and the flank offsets use the tolerance
  magnification ×k, so the drawn interference (magenta) and clearance (green)
  can be compared with the drawn tolerances. The nut does not move.
- Hex head and nut proportions are approximate heavy-hex dimensions
  (across flats ≈ 1.5D + 1/8 in), chamfered 30°, for visualisation only.
